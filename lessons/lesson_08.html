Phase 1
Bold, Italic, and Underline
💡 Concept

HTML has specific tags for formatting text. <strong> makes text bold and important (search engines pay attention to strong text). <em> makes text italic and emphasised. <u> underlines text. There are also older tags: <b> (bold without importance) and <i> (italic without emphasis). Use <strong> and <em> for content that matters, and <b> and <i> just for styling.

index.html 
<!DOCTYPE html>
<html lang="en">
  <head><title>Text Formatting</title></head>
  <body>

    <h1>Text Formatting Examples</h1>

    <p>This word is <strong>very important</strong> — use strong!</p>

    <p>This is <em>emphasised</em> — use em for emphasis!</p>

    <p>Old style: <b>bold</b> and <i>italic</i> — still work but
       strong and em are better.</p>

    <p>This text has an <u>underline</u>.</p>

    <p>You can <strong><em>combine bold and italic</em></strong> too!</p>

  </body>
</html>

Phase 2
Mark, Small, Del, and Sub/Sup
💡 Concept

HTML has more formatting tags: <mark> highlights text in yellow like a marker pen. <small> makes text smaller. <del> shows deleted/crossed-out text (like a price reduction). <ins> shows inserted text. <sub> makes subscript (H₂O). <sup> makes superscript (E=mc²). These are all semantic tags — they carry meaning!

index.html 
<!DOCTYPE html>
<html lang="en">
  <head><title>More Formatting</title></head>
  <body>

    <h1>More Text Formatting</h1>

    <p>Don't miss this <mark>important announcement!</mark></p>

    <p><small>Terms and conditions apply. Offer ends Friday.</small></p>

    <p>Old price: <del>₦5,000</del> New price: <ins>₦3,500</ins></p>

    <p>Water is written as H<sub>2</sub>O in chemistry.</p>

    <p>Einstein's famous formula: E = mc<sup>2</sup></p>

    <p>This is <strong><mark>bold and highlighted</mark></strong>!</p>

  </body>
</html>

Phase 3
Combining Formatting Tags
💡 Concept

The real power comes from combining formatting tags. You can have bold highlighted text, or a subscript inside an important formula. Remember the nesting rule: whatever opens last must close first. Good structure: <strong><mark>text</mark></strong> ✅. Bad nesting: <strong><mark>text</strong></mark> ✗.

index.html 
<!DOCTYPE html>
<html lang="en">
  <head><title>Combining Tags</title></head>
  <body>

    <h1>Science Notebook</h1>

    <p>Water (H<sub>2</sub>O) is <strong>essential</strong> for life.
       <mark>We need 8 glasses per day!</mark></p>

    <p>The speed of light is approximately <strong>3 × 10<sup>8</sup></strong>
       metres per second.</p>

    <p><em><mark>Don't forget</mark></em> to drink water!</p>

    <p><small>Source: Science textbook, Chapter 3</small></p>

  </body>
</html>

My Formatted Article

<html lang="en">
  <head>
    <title>Tech News — Formatted Article</title>
  </head>
  <body style="font-family: Georgia, serif; max-width: 700px;
               margin: 0 auto; padding: 30px;">

    <h1>Breaking News: <strong>HTML Learner Builds First Website!</strong></h1>

    <p><small>Published: Monday, Ibadan | By [Your Name]</small></p>
    <hr>

    <p>
      A student at <strong>Techbase Academy</strong> has successfully
      built their <em>very first</em> website using <mark>pure HTML</mark>.
    </p>

    <p>
      The project started at <del>₦10,000</del> but was completed for
      just <ins>₦0</ins> — because HTML is <strong>completely free!</strong>
    </p>

    <p>
      Did you know? HTML<sup>5</sup> is the 5th version of HTML, released
      in 2014. Water (H<sub>2</sub>O) and HTML share one thing in common —
      both are <em><mark>essential</mark></em> for modern life!
    </p>

    <hr>
    <p><small>© 2025 Techbase HTML Learner | This is a practice article</small></p>

  </body>
</html>
